# Copilot-in-PowerPoint prompts — Part 3: "Could vs. Should"

Six prompts, one per slide. Run them **one at a time** in the Copilot pane ("Add a slide…"),
review the result, then move to the next. That's Rule 1 in action — right-size the ask.
Copilot follows *content* faithfully but treats *layout* as a suggestion, so expect to
hand-tune positioning and colors afterward. That's Rule 2 — the output is a draft; make it yours.

---

## Prompt 1 — Section divider

```
Add a section divider slide with a dark navy background. Small amber label at top
center: "PART 3". Large centered title: "Could vs. Should". Centered subtitle below:
"There is plenty AI could do in our work. The question a risk team is built to ask
is what it should do — and how." No other content on the slide.
```

## Prompt 2 — Two traps

```
Add a slide titled "Two traps these tools invite" with the subtitle "Easy to fall
into, and both undermine the work." Two side-by-side cards.

Left card — small label "THE INPUT TRAP", heading "Dumping everything in", body:
"It's tempting to pour endless context and instructions into the tool and expect a
clean answer back. Overloaded asks breed errors and contradictions — and the output
comes back polished enough to hide them."

Right card — small label "THE OUTPUT TRAP", heading "Taking output as finished",
body: "AI output cannot be relied on as written. It's a draft — unverified, unowned,
in nobody's voice. The moment it moves forward untouched, its errors become your errors."

Across the bottom, a full-width dark navy banner with white bold text: "AI is meant
to enhance your work — not be a crutch. The work, and the responsibility for it,
stay yours."
```

## Prompt 3 — What AI is and isn't

```
Add a slide titled "The root: what AI is — and isn't" with the subtitle "Both traps
come from treating AI like a colleague who gets it." Two columns.

Left column headed "It is", with three items: "A tireless reader and a fast, fluent
drafter" / "A pattern engine — output only as good as the instructions and sources
you feed it" / "Equally confident-sounding when it's right and when it's wrong".

Right column headed "It isn't", with three items: "Aware of what you meant but
didn't say" / "Able to catch its own errors — or tell you your instructions
contradict each other" / "The author of your work — it can't own a conclusion or
sign a workpaper".

Bottom banner in dark navy with white text: "It's a drafting engine — and it depends
on you at both ends: the ask, and the edit."
```

## Prompt 4 — Rule 1

```
Add a slide with a small amber label "RULE 1 · THE ASK END", title "Right-size the
ask", subtitle "How to give AI work it can actually do well". Four cards in a 2x2 grid:

1. "One task per prompt" — "If a prompt needs a table of contents, it's a project,
   not a prompt. Chain small steps — draft, check, refine — instead of one giant pass."
2. "Contradictions fail silently" — "The model won't tell you your instructions
   conflict. It quietly picks one and keeps writing — and the output still reads clean."
3. "The new-hire test" — "Could a sharp new hire follow the ask without a single
   question back? If not, AI can't either — it just won't say so."
4. "Prompts are work products" — "Anything distributed to the team deserves review
   before it ships — prompts included. That's exactly what the prompt library is for."
```

## Prompt 5 — Rule 2

```
Add a slide with a small amber label "RULE 2 · THE EDIT END", title "Own the output",
subtitle "The agent drafts. You author." Four cards in a 2x2 grid:

1. "It's raw material" — "Every agent draft is a first pass from something that has
   never met your reviewee, your standards, or your QC."
2. "Make it yours" — "Name the actual documents. Use workpaper language. Rewrite
   generic AI phrasing into the sentence you would have written."
3. "The signature test" — "If it goes to QC under your name, you own every sentence
   in it — the ones you wrote and the ones you didn't."
4. "QC is not your editor" — "Forwarding raw output doesn't save the work — it moves
   it to a teammate's desk, with your name still on it."

Bottom banner in dark navy with white text: "The agent does the assembly. The
reviewer does the judgment. Skipping the judgment isn't using the agent — it's
forwarding its homework."
```

## Prompt 6 — The quick test

```
Add a slide titled "The quick test" with the subtitle "Before AI touches a task, two
questions place it — how easily can you check it, and what happens if it's wrong?"
Show a 2x2 matrix. Column headers: "Easy to verify" and "Hard to verify". Row labels:
"Low stakes" and "High stakes".

Top-left cell: "Delegate freely — summaries, drafts, formatting. Example: the Power
Prompt library."
Top-right cell: "Use, but sample — brainstorming and research starting points;
spot-check before you rely on them."
Bottom-left cell (highlight this one in dark navy): "AI drafts, you approve — the
sweet spot for review work. Example: the Review Agent's workpapers."
Bottom-right cell: "Keep it human-led — final conclusions, ratings, judgment calls.
AI supports — it doesn't decide."

Below the matrix, small italic text: "Try it: pick one task from your own week and
place it on this grid."
```

---

### One-shot version (if you want to test the contrast)

Paste all six slide descriptions above into a single prompt prefixed with "Add six
slides to this presentation:" — then compare the result against the one-at-a-time
run. If the one-shot comes back messier, you have a live demonstration of Rule 1
for the team.

### Tips

- Run prompts in an existing deck that already uses your theme — Copilot inherits it.
- Ask "add speaker notes summarizing this slide in two sentences" as a follow-up per
  slide if you want notes; results vary.
- Copilot may ignore the navy/amber color instructions — recolor by hand or drop them
  and let your template drive.
